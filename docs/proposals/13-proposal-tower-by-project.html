Switch by project, not by lens

The three tabs split the screen by way of looking. You think in projects. So every question about one app currently means reading three tabs and filtering each one by eye.

“Rather than switching board, ledger and progress, I should be able to switch between projects with all details.”
Why the lenses existed, and why they stop being needed

BOARD, LEDGER and PROGRESS were a response to a real constraint: six projects' features, history and flow do not fit in 900px, so the estate view had to be cut three ways.

One project's data fits easily. Measured on the live tree:

Filtered to one app, everything fits on one screen at once — so the lenses can go entirely rather than being re-cut per project. The split was never the right answer; it was the answer to a volume problem that disappears the moment the axis changes.

What changes

The tab bar becomes the project list

ALL · pockets · finance-tracker · pip · mac-explorer · common-rules · idea-lab, carried in the URL as ?project=pockets for the same reason the view tabs were — the page re-requests itself every 10 seconds and DOM state does not survive that. Measured when the view tabs were built, and it applies unchanged.

A project page shows everything about that project

Its features by state, its burnup and velocity, its sessions, its queued and merged work — all at once, no second click. This is the thing that is currently impossible: “how is pockets doing” takes three tab switches and three visual filters, and the answer is never on one screen.

NEEDS YOU stays global, above the tabs

It is the one region that must not be filtered. “What needs me across everything” is the autopilot's blocking state, and hiding part of it behind a project tab would mean a decision waiting on you in an app you are not currently looking at is invisible. It keeps its place above the tab bar, and it keeps counting every project.

ALL becomes the estate summary

One line per project: features by state, completion, velocity, sessions, whether it is behind the rules. The cross-project glance you occasionally want, rather than the only view on offer.

Worth saying plainly

The ALL page is, almost exactly, the Portfolio option from proposal 11 — the one I recommended and you turned down in favour of Ledger and Board. It was the wrong recommendation then: as the only view it hides individual features behind an expand, and with one register declared it had nothing to compare. As a summary sitting above per-project pages it is the right shape, because comparison is exactly what a top level is for.

A project page · features, progress, flow and sessions together · NEEDS YOU above the tabs and never filtered

What this costs

  • A cross-project feature list disappears. The LEDGER's one-table view of every feature everywhere goes away. The ALL page compares projects, not features. With one register declared that costs nothing today; with six it might, and it can come back as a row on ALL if it turns out to be missed.
  • More clicks for a cross-project sweep. “Show me everything blocked, anywhere” becomes six tab visits. That is the trade you are making, and it is the right one if the common question is about one app.
  • The tab bar grows with the estate. Six projects fit; twelve would need scrolling or grouping. Worth knowing before the estate doubles.

What does not change

  • Everything built today survives — the board, the burnup, the strip, the staleness line, the contested filter, the decision queue. This is a navigation change: the same renderers, filtered to one project and composed on one page.
  • ?project= is URL-carried for the reason ?view= was: DOM state does not survive the 10-second refresh. Measured, and unchanged.
  • No new region, no forecast, no new bookkeeping.
