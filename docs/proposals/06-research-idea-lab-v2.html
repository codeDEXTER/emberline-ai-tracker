The Idea Lab — refining an idea until it deserves your attention

Your ask: "I can just tell the idea. It should run multiple iterations to make the idea finer and finer, and then let me know." You called it a v2 of common rules and want it kept separate for now. This is the research — what exists, what the evidence says works and fails, and four architectures with a recommendation. Nothing here is a build.

The one finding that shapes everythingdesign constraint, not detail
An idea polishing itself gets worse, not finer

The evidence is consistent and recent: LLMs cannot reliably find their own mistakes without external signals (Huang et al., ICLR 2024 — they can correct a flagged error robustly, but cannot detect it). Naive self-refinement plateaus after 3–5 rounds, anchors on its own early framing, and — measured on research ideation — successive generations grow more similar, not more diverse. "Run more iterations" alone produces confident convergence on the first draft's assumptions.

So the whole design question becomes: where does the external signal come from each round? Every credible system answers with some mix of four things — fresh evidence (search, data), genuinely separate critics, competition between alternatives, and periodic human taste. The architectures below differ mainly in which they lean on.

What's out therethe state of the art, honestly reported

Critic–refiner loops — Self-Refine, Reflexion

One agent drafts, a critic critiques, the draft is revised. Cheap, well-studied, and the producer/critic separation is what carries it — self-critique alone is the unreliable part. Gains level off in a handful of rounds. This is the floor, not the architecture.

Persona panels and debate

Several agents with genuinely different stances argue an idea, then converge by vote or judge. The empirical results are useful and specific: diversity of output is bounded by who is in the room, not how long they talk — persona composition is the single most consequential design choice — and when every persona is the same underlying model with a thin mask, performance can drop below a single agent. Debate adds real signal only when the perspectives are truly distinct (different briefs, different evidence, different incentives).

Tournament + evolution — Google's Co-Scientist, AlphaEvolve, FunSearch

The strongest published results. Co-Scientist (Nature, May 2026 — lab-validated in drug repurposing) runs a population of hypotheses through pairwise Elo debates; winners are evolved into variants that re-enter the tournament; a meta-review agent adjusts the other agents' prompts between rounds. AlphaEvolve/FunSearch do the evolutionary version for code, with an archive keeping diverse candidates alive rather than only the best. Two lessons transfer directly: compare ideas against each other, not against a rubric (pairwise beats absolute scoring), and keep the weird ones — a pure leaderboard collapses diversity, which is the raw material of refinement.

Full autonomous pipelines — Sakana's AI Scientist

The cautionary tale. It claims idea-to-paper autonomy; independent evaluation found a 42% experimental failure rate, novelty checks fooled by keyword-level literature search, and hallucinated results presented as findings. The lesson is not "impossible" but "don't remove the human from taste and truth." LLM-generated ideas score high on novelty and low on feasibility; your job in the loop is exactly the judgement they lack.

Four architecturesoptions, then a recommendation

A · The Polisher

critic–refiner loop, 3–5 rounds, distinct critic lenses

Your idea is drafted up, then cycled through separate critics — feasibility, novelty, "what breaks", cost — with a revision after each round, stopping when a round produces no new objections. One session, minutes to an hour.

cheapestbuildable today with existing agentsplateaus fastanchors on your first framing — refines the idea you had, never finds the better adjacent one

B · The Roundtable

persona panel debates, then synthesises

Five-ish genuinely different stances — the sceptical CFO, the user's advocate, the engineer who maintains it, the competitor, the person who says "this exists, here's the link" — each armed with its own web research, debating your idea over a few rounds before one synthesis.

surfaces objections you'd meet months laterdiverse by constructionquality hangs entirely on persona compositionconverges by consensus, which sands off bold edges

C · The Tournament

co-scientist pattern: population → pairwise Elo → evolve → repeat

Your idea is deliberately re-interpreted into 8–12 variants (including ones you didn't mean). Pairs debate; an Elo ladder forms; the top few are mutated and crossed into new variants that re-enter; an archive protects the odd ones. After N generations you see the ladder, the survivors, and what died and why.

strongest published resultsescapes your first framing — finds the adjacent better ideapairwise judging is the most reliable LLM evaluation knownmost expensive by faroverkill for small ideas

D · The Maturation Funnel

stage-gate: expand → ground → stress → converge, one look per stage

Fixed stages rather than free iteration: first expand (many interpretations), then ground (research each against reality — precedent, competitors, feasibility), then stress (adversarial critics try to kill the survivors), then converge (synthesis with a recommendation). You can look between stages or only at the end.

predictable cost and durationmaps cleanly onto your three-looks philosophyevery round has external signal by constructionrigid — a stage runs even when it has nothing to add

E · Recommended — the Funnel with a Tournament heart

D's stages, C's selection inside them, sized to the idea

Expand your one-sentence idea into a population of genuinely different interpretations. Ground each with real research — this is where fresh external signal enters, every round, which is the non-negotiable the evidence demands. Select by pairwise comparison, keeping an archive of the interesting losers. Evolve the winners and re-enter them — but only while rounds still change the ranking; two static rounds ends it, the same loop-until-dry bound as everywhere else in the rules. Then tell you: one document — the refined idea, the two strongest rivals, what was tried and killed, and the open questions only you can answer. Small ideas get one pass through; big ones get generations.

How it stays separate from v1 — and where it touchesyour constraint, kept
  • Its own place — an idea-lab/ alongside common-rules, not inside it. Its own rules file, its own agents if needed. Nothing in v1 changes.
  • One touchpoint only: the Lab's output is proposal-shaped. When you like a refined idea, it enters v1 at L1 as a normal proposal — the Lab replaces the blank page, not the pipeline.
  • It runs in management sessions, which is where the rules already put research — before a direction exists, while it can still change.
  • Your involvement matches proposal 04's philosophy: tell the idea, then one look at the end — with the option of a mid-funnel look for big ideas. The AI decides everything inside the funnel and records what it killed and why.
What the Lab cannot do, stated now

It cannot supply taste — the evidence is blunt that LLM ideas skew novel-but-infeasible, and the feasibility judgement stays with you and with the grounding research. It cannot verify its own novelty claims from keyword search alone (Sakana's documented failure), so "this exists already" checks must fetch and read, not just search. And its cost is real: a full tournament pass is tens of agent runs. Sizing the machinery to the idea is a rule, not an optimisation.

Open questionsfor when you pick a direction
Where does an idea enter? A dedicated Idea Lab session per idea, or any management session that hears "here's an idea" and routes it. The second is more natural; the first is more controllable.
Do killed ideas stay findable? The archive of losers is the Lab's memory — next quarter's idea may be last quarter's loser whose time came. Costs storage and an index; probably worth it.
Mid-funnel look: opt-in or default for big ideas? One look at the end is the purest version of your ask. A look after Ground, before the tournament spends real money, is the cheap insurance.
