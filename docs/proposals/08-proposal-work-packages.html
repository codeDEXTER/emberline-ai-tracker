Work packages — how much of each app is done, and when it will be

Your ask: track work packages as collections of features, with progress and time to completion, and say how far along each feature and each app actually is. This proposal is mostly a finding: the register you want already exists and is already maintained by hand — four apps carry one. It is prose, so nothing reads it. Below is what is derivable today, what is genuinely missing, the screen drawn with real numbers, and the four rule changes it needs.

What is there todaymeasured 2026-08-05, not estimated

The finding

CLAUDE-checklist.md already is a work-package register. Its ## headings are packages — "Now — small, ready to pick up", "Next — Detail-pages redesign", "Capture & local-AI reevaluation" — and every item is already a - [x] or - [ ]. That is a collection of features with a completion state per item, written down, kept current, in four apps. It is not machine-read by anything.

So this is not a proposal to build a register. It is a proposal to read the one you already keep, and to close the one join that is missing.

The one thing that is genuinely missing

A package can be counted but not followed, because a checklist item rarely names its issue — and the issue is what connects to a branch, a session and a merge date.

Time to completionwhat the arithmetic can and cannot honestly say

Two clocks exist, and they measure different things. Keeping them apart is the whole design.

SourceGivesDoes not give
GitHub issuesreal timestamps — cycle time and throughputa stable denominator: scope grows as issues are cut, so "% of issues closed" rises and falls for reasons that are not progress
the checklistthe denominator — your own statement of intended scope, including work not yet cut into issuesany timestamp at all: a ticked box records that something is done, never when

So: the denominator comes from the checklist, the rate comes from issues, and the two are never silently mixed. Measured rate, finance-tracker: 18 issues closed across 4 active days — 4, 3, 3, 8 — a median of 3.5 per active day, with a median cycle time of 0.76 days from cut to closed (n=18). Applied to 27 remaining checklist items, that is roughly 8 active days.

Three caveats that belong on the screen, not in a footnote

An active day is not a calendar day. Four active days is the entire history. A week where you do not work on it is not in the model, and the figure must never render as a date.

Checklist items are not issues. 44 items against 43 issues is a coincidence, not a mapping — only 20% are linked. Until step 2 below, the rate is borrowed from a different population than the denominator, and the screen should say "at the recent pace" rather than imply a computed forecast.

Three apps have no closure history at all. pockets, pip and mac-explorer have closed zero issues, so they get a completion figure and no rate. Showing "no rate yet" is correct; inventing one from another app's velocity is not.

What "degree of completion" honestly means

  • Feature — closed over total issues in the group. Available only where a feature is actually declared, which today is three groups across six apps.
  • Package — ticked over total items in a ## section. Available today, for four apps, with no change to anything.
  • App — the same sum across its packages. Available today, same four apps.

And what it is not: a percentage of the app being finished. It is the fraction of declared scope that is done. Adding five items to a package moves its bar backwards while nothing got worse — which is honest, and should be visible rather than smoothed away.

What this needs from the shared rulesyou asked — four changes, three small
1 · The checklist gets a declared shape

One ## heading per work package, one - [ ] per item, and the issue number in parentheses when an issue exists. This is already the convention in finance-tracker; writing it down makes it parseable rather than habitual. Amends the checklist paragraph in each project's CLAUDE.md plus a line in CLAUDE-workflow.md.

2 · Every project carries one, or is explicitly exempt

common-rules and idea-lab have no checklist, so they have no declared scope and cannot report completion. Either they get one or the rule states they are exempt and why — an app with no answer is fine, an app with a silently missing answer is not.

3 · An item names its issue once an issue exists

The join, and the only genuinely new obligation here. It is 0% in three of four projects today. Without it, packages can be counted but never followed, and every "in flight" figure on the drawn screen stays empty.

4 · Ticking the box moves into code

bin/land ticks the item when the branch merges, rather than a session remembering to. This follows the rule the file already states — "rules that can be enforced are not written here, they live in bin/derecord" — and it has a second effect that matters more than the tidiness: a tick written by land carries a date. That is the timestamp the checklist does not have, and it is what turns the borrowed rate above into a real measured one.

Changing these files is reserved to you. Nothing here edits a rule; this proposal is the surfacing step, and the changes above would come as their own PR after a decision.

Build paththree steps, each worth having alone
Step 1 — read what already exists

Parse the four checklists, add the APPS & WORK PACKAGES region to the Tower. Answers "how far along is each app" immediately, needs no rule change and no new file, and is roughly the size of the change that just landed as #35. Everything on the drawn screen except the "in flight" column comes from this step.

Step 2 — close the join

Rule changes 1 and 3, then land ticking boxes with a date (change 4). Packages become followable: which item is in flight, in which session, and when the last one landed. This is also what starts accumulating honest velocity data.

Step 3 — time to completion from measured velocity

Once ticks carry dates, per-package throughput is real rather than borrowed from issue closures, and a range can be quoted per package instead of one number for the whole app. Deliberately last: quoting a forecast before step 2 means quoting a rate measured on a different population.

Honest limitswhat this will not do, at any step
  • It measures declared scope. An app at 39% is 39% of what has been written down, and what gets written down grows as you learn what the app is. This never becomes a percentage of "finished".
  • It cannot see unwritten work. A package that exists only in your head is not on the screen, and its absence looks exactly like being done.
  • Parsing makes a prose file load-bearing. The checklist is currently free to be as loose as it likes; the moment the Tower renders figures from it, a malformed heading becomes a wrong number on a screen. Step 1 should render what it could not parse, rather than dropping it silently.
  • Four active days is a thin base for any forecast, and three apps have none. The ETA column earns its place only after step 2.
The decisionyours

Step 1 stands on its own, changes no rule, and gives you the completion figures for four apps. Steps 2 and 3 need the four rule changes above, which are yours to accept — and step 3 is worth nothing without step 2.
