The Tower keeps the record — so it can say how done the work is, and whether it still needs you

Two lines of work arrived at the same gap from opposite directions. One asked how complete is each app, and when will it be done. The other — the Idea Lab's funnel on the Tower, which you accepted into v1 — asked what else could the Tower be, and answered: it should keep the record and report whether the autopilot still needs you. Neither can be built, because nothing in the toolchain writes down when anything happened. This is the combined proposal: one durable record, three readings of it, and the four rule changes both halves need.

Why one document and not two

Proposal 08 measures the work — declared scope, completion per package, time to done. The Lab's funnel measures the collaboration — how often you were needed, what it cost, whether that is falling. Different questions, and both were sound standing alone.

They meet at exactly one point. 08's step 2 was "have bin/land tick the checklist box so the tick carries a date". The funnel's winner was "append one dated row every time the Tower re-renders". These are the same move — one made for a single file, one made in general. Two independent runs found the same missing thing without knowing about each other, which is the strongest evidence either produced. Building them separately would mean writing the timestamp machinery twice and then reconciling two records that disagree.

The fact that reorganises bothverified on your disk, twice, not quoted from a doc

Everything the Tower reads is on a delete timer, and your entire history is six days old.

The Lab's funnel measured 282 files / 536 MB a couple of hours earlier; this run measured 289 / 512 MB. Both are right — it is growing and being rewritten while we look at it. The figure that matters is not the size, it is the date: ~30 August.

The obvious cheap answer, and why it is only half an answer

Set cleanupPeriodDays to something large today. It is one line, it is reversible, and it costs nothing — you should do it whatever you decide below, as insurance. Do not let this proposal be the reason the 31 July sessions are lost.

But it is not a substitute. It preserves 512 MB of private conversation that grows ~85 MB a day and that concept 07's own limit says must never leave the machine. A derived one-line-per-event record is small, greppable, permanent, safe to show a room of managers, and — unlike a transcript — something you can actually plot. Keep the raw data by setting the key; build the record because the record is the thing you can read.

Reading two · the report cardthe question the four current regions cannot answer

The Tower answers "how is it handling things right now" well. It cannot answer "is this needing me less than it did last week", because that needs yesterday and yesterday is not kept. Five kinds of row — all already computed every five seconds and then discarded — carry it: an issue changed state, a session handed work to another, something landed on the wall, you cleared it, work merged. The cost figure bin/spend derives and nothing preserves becomes a column.

Grounding found there is a published way to measure this rather than inventing one: Anthropic's own autonomy work uses auto-approve rate, interrupt frequency and turn duration, and found Claude asks for clarification more than twice as often as humans interrupt it. That turns your standing complaint — "stop making me the mechanism" — into a number that can be plotted.

Time to completionwhat the arithmetic can honestly say, and what the record would fix
SourceGivesDoes not give
GitHub issuesreal timestamps — cycle time and throughputa stable denominator: cutting an issue moves the percentage for reasons that are not progress
the checklistthe denominator — your own statement of scope, including work not yet cut into issuesany timestamp at all: a ticked box records that something is done, never when
the logbookboth, joined — a dated row per state change, attributable to a packagea past. It starts empty, and nothing recovers what is already gone

Until the logbook has history, the denominator comes from the checklist and the rate from issues, and the two are never silently mixed. Measured rate, finance-tracker: 18 issues closed over 4 active days — 4, 3, 3, 8 — a median of 3.5 per active day, median cycle time 0.76 days (n=18). Against 27 remaining checklist items, roughly 8 active days.

Three caveats that belong on the screen, not in a footnote

An active day is not a calendar day. Four active days is the entire history. The figure must never render as a date.

Checklist items are not issues. 44 items against 43 issues is a coincidence, not a mapping — only 20% are linked in finance-tracker and 0% in the other three. Until the join closes, the rate is borrowed from a different population than the denominator, and the screen should say "at the recent pace" rather than imply a forecast.

Three apps have no closure history at all. pockets, pip and mac-explorer have closed zero issues. They get a completion figure and no rate. "No rate yet" is correct; borrowing another app's velocity is not.

Every one of those caveats is the same shortage: no dated history. The logbook is what retires them — not by being clever, but by having been running for a month.

What this needs from the shared rulesfour changes — the same four serve both readings
1 · The checklist gets a declared shape

One ## per work package, one - [ ] per item, issue number in parentheses when one exists. Already the convention in finance-tracker; writing it down makes it parseable rather than habitual.

2 · Every project carries one, or is explicitly exempt

common-rules and idea-lab have none, so they have no declared scope and cannot report completion. An app with no answer is fine; an app with a silently missing answer is not.

3 · An item names its issue once an issue exists

The join. The only genuinely new obligation here, and 0% in three of four projects today.

4 · Writing the record is a standing obligation, not a habit

bin/land ticks the box and appends the logbook row on merge, rather than a session remembering to. This follows the rule the file already states — enforceable rules live in code, not prose. Be clear-eyed that this is the real cost of the proposal: something must write the record on every render, forever, and it becomes load-bearing the moment a number on a screen depends on it.

Changing these files is reserved to you. Nothing here edits a rule; this document is the surfacing step.

Build pathresequenced — the record is first because it is the only part on a clock

One line, reversible, no code. Independent of every decision below. The 31 July sessions are deleted around 30 August otherwise.

Step 1a · start the logbook

Append the five row kinds on every Tower render. Nothing reads it yet. It is first because a logbook started in September has no August — every day of delay is a day permanently absent from every trend that follows.

Step 1b · the burn-up, in parallel

Parse the four checklists, add the completion region. Independent of 1a, needs no rule change, and gives you the figures in this document on a live screen. Roughly the size of the change that landed as #35 today.

Step 2 · close the join

Rule changes 1–3, then land writing the tick and the row together. Packages become followable and logbook rows become attributable. This is the step that makes both readings more than counting.

Step 3 · the report card, once there is a month to report on

Deliberately last, and gated on elapsed time rather than on effort. Drawing a confident slope through four points is worse than drawing nothing.

What grounding killedfrom the Lab's funnel — fetched and read, not searched
  • "Live cost instrument" — killed. agents-observe, claude-code-otel and three others already show live events, tool calls, tokens and cost, better than we would. None has a queue of things waiting on a human, an edge between two sessions, or any link to an issue. The Tower's wall and graph are the genuinely unusual parts.
  • "Answer sessions from the Tower" and "ship the wall as a phone app" — killed. Remote Control is first-party now: drive a local session from your phone, with a push toggle for permission prompts and questions. Anthropic ships the delivery; its session list is flat, so the content of the wall is still yours.
  • "Productise the Tower" — killed. Vibe Kanban, Conductor and Nimbalyst own that category, and they own the work they launch. The Tower observes work it did not start.
  • Two rivals kept, not killed. The Tower's reader is the autopilot, not you — make the graph an API and the screen a side effect; held back only because a window every session depends on is load-bearing infrastructure with a much higher bar than a read-only page. And decision provenance — "when did we decide that, and why", across ASKS, CHANGELOG, AGENT-LOG and transcripts; it needs the logbook first, so it is worth revisiting after step 1a.
Honest limitswhat this will not do, at any step
  • A logbook started today has no yesterday. Everything before 31 July is already gone. The trend line starts thin and stays thin for weeks; the first honest report card is a month away.
  • Six days is not a baseline. Any autonomy or velocity figure computed now is a number, not a trend. Both halves of this proposal hit the same wall and both must say "no rate yet" rather than draw a slope.
  • It measures what it can count. A day with no wall items might be good autonomy or a day you were out. The record cannot tell those apart.
  • Completion measures declared scope, and what gets declared grows as you learn what an app is. This never becomes a percentage of "finished", and it cannot see work that exists only in your head — whose absence looks exactly like being done.
  • Parsing makes prose load-bearing. The checklist is currently free to be loose; the moment figures are rendered from it, a malformed heading is a wrong number on a screen. Step 1b should show what it could not parse rather than dropping it silently.
Open questionsonly you can answer these — the Lab is not allowed to
  • What counts as an interruption that should not have happened? A permission prompt, a clarifying question and a Look are three different things, and the report card is worthless until you say which are legitimate.
  • One surface or two? A live window and a historical record want opposite things from a screen. A fifth region is cheaper; a separate quiet surface is more honest.
  • Is the record worth the standing obligation? Something must write it forever, and it becomes load-bearing the moment a number depends on it. The alternative is accepting that this month disappears.
