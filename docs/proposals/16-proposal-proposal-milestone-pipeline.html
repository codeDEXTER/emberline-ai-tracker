A proposal declares the milestones it creates, and its status follows from them

proposeddesign-conceptProposal 16 · topic: proposal-milestone-pipeline · redrafted 23 August 2026 after the pilot

The sponsor's observation, 2026-08-20: "each proposal is a feature where all the decisions it needs to do in a pipeline are different milestones." The first draft argued it was true for one class of proposal and false for another. A pilot against finance-tracker's real plan, and a survey of this repo's own, then measured it — and the measurement changed three of the six decisions. This redraft is what survived.

What this proposes An accepted proposal that authorises building declares the milestones it creates. Only its sequencing decisions become stages; most are parameters, and a fifth of them are neither — a third answer the first draft did not have. Milestones may still arise with no proposal. A proposal's terminal status is computed from its milestones rather than typed — across the whole terminal vocabulary, with an empty declaration computing to unanswerable rather than to success.
What the measurement cost the first draft D2 was binary and should not have been — 19% of real decisions sort into neither bucket, and they cluster in every proposal that contains sequencing thinking at all. D4 was too narrow — it derived two words out of a vocabulary of seven, and the status that actually lost work was one of the five it ignored. D5's premise was right and its target was wrong — the checker it proposes would be called and would always skip, in both repos measured.

The observation holds three for three, and this part is unchanged

Every accepted proposal that authorised building produced an ordered chain, and its own decisions fixed the order:

ProposalThe decisions that set an orderBecame
24 · design system D2 tokens alone → D3 migrate page by page → D5 dark mode only after #580 → #532 → #531
23 · the work light D5 — the event-loop stalls land before the footer #537 → #536
25 · feature boundaries D3 — surfaces, then engines, then channels that order, held

In each case the order was argued in the proposal and then obeyed by the work. That is a pipeline of milestones, and the sponsor's observation names something real.

But the test is not binary measured 2026-08-23, and this is new

The first draft offered one test: does this decision answer "what must be true before the next thing starts"? If yes it is a stage; if it sets a parameter of what gets built, it is not. The pilot applied that test to every decision in every accepted finance-tracker proposal. The result:

VerdictCountShare
Stages622%
Parameters1659%
Neither519%
Total decisions examined27across 5 proposals

The distribution matters more than the total, and it is the bad kind. The five do not sit together in one loosely-written document. They fall in three of the five proposals — specifically, in every proposal that contains any sequencing thinking at all. The two proposals with zero unclassifiable decisions (18, 20) are pure parameters end to end. So this is a blind spot in the test, not carelessness in the writing.

Three shapes recur, and none of them is exotic:

  • The adopt decision (23·D1, 24·D1) — "do this at all". It gates everything downstream, yet produces no row of its own.
  • The standing gate (23·D4, 25·D4) — a rule that must hold before and during every later stage. Both answer "what must be true before the next thing starts" verbatim, and neither yields a milestone. 25·D4 is answered no, which a stage cannot be.
  • The process decision (25·D5) — who writes the thing, rather than what gets written.
The test misfires on this proposal's own worked example The first draft classified 23·D4 as a parameter, in the table it used to demonstrate the test. Under the test as stated, 23·D4 answers the stage question word for word. It is a standing gate, which is neither — and the draft reached the right answer by instinct while its own stated rule pointed elsewhere. A rule that its author cannot apply correctly to their own example is not yet a rule.

So the test keeps its usefulness and loses its authority. It is a good heuristic and a bad gate. Forcing an ambiguous decision to one side is not harmless: the pilot did exactly that on its own restructure and produced a milestone row citing two parameters as its source. Three buckets, and the third is a real answer rather than a failure to decide.

And it does not generalise two ways it breaks

Not every decision is a stage

Proposal 24's D6 keeps the palette picker; D4 makes the tracked-scope test a hard gate. These change what gets built, not when. Recorded in the Decided block, consumed by whoever implements, never a row in a plan.

Not every proposal produces milestones

Proposal 22's D1 was "No new timing-test work. The audit is the deliverable." A research proposal whose honest conclusion is do not build this. It creates zero milestones, and no amount of waiting will change that. A rule demanding milestones from every proposal would push that proposal to invent some.

The first draft said proposal 22 "is complete". It is not — it still reads proposed. The example survives; the claim did not. Recorded rather than quietly deleted, because it is this proposal's own thesis happening to this proposal: an asserted status going stale inside the document arguing that asserted statuses go stale. Found by the pilot, not by review.

Not every milestone has a proposal

finance-tracker's money-correctness milestone came from a quality review. Its performance-architecture milestone came from the sponsor directly. The pilot measured the ratio: of 16 milestone rows, 12 trace to exactly one proposal, 1 to two, and 3 to none. The three with none include the two hardest rows in the plan. A rule requiring a proposal per milestone charges ceremony against exactly the work that least needs it.

The cost case why this is not a tidiness proposal

Everything above argues from housekeeping. Three days after the first draft, an audit of finance-tracker's Spending area produced the argument from damage.

Proposal 01, One book for the household, is stamped built. Its Workstream 3 says, verbatim:

"The single largest genuine gap: the app has no concept of income. Inflow categories (salary, interest, refund, FD maturity, transfer-in) and a transfer category on the expense side… On top of that, a savings rate, and the sentence you actually asked for, generated as a monthly household statement and archived."

The transfer half shipped, as flows.py. The income half never did. Nothing has tracked the remainder since, because the proposal reads finished. CLAUDE-checklist.md admits this about itself: "proposal 01's two orphans (the income model and the retirement half — neither is tracked anywhere since 01 was marked built)". A search of every issue in that repo for income, salary and savings rate returned nothing.

What surfaced it, and what did not Not a review, not a checker, not this proposal. The sponsor asked in August 2026 for a feature — total income, income by kind, a figure he could plan an investment against — and only then did anyone discover it had already been accepted, scoped in detail, and marked done. Throughout, the project's ordinary machinery ran and reported nothing wrong, because by every record it consults, nothing was. The work was not deprioritised, argued down, or deferred. It was lost, by a status.

And the first draft's rule would not have caught it. Proposal 01 is built — outside the completed / completed in part derivation entirely. It also declares no milestones, so vacuously all of none are done: under D4 as first written it computes to completed and stays exactly as wrong as it is today. The cost case is evidence for derivation and a correction to it in the same breath.

Filed as finance-tracker issue #624, so the capability is tracked whatever is decided here — the harm is repaired independently of the rule. The same audit found that feature's milestone row, its register entry and its closed issue #254 disagreeing with each other about its state, which is the identical defect one level down.

What it buys a hole opened 2026-08-19, closed properly this time

The payoff is not tidiness. It is that terminal status becomes derivable: every declared milestone done → completed; some → completed in part, with the unfinished rows as the exceptions list. Today completed in part depends on a person remembering to enumerate what was skipped — the same discipline dependency one level up. Derived, it cannot be fudged by omission.

The pilot tested this against the record and it is the strongest positive result in the whole exercise. Derived status disagreed with asserted status on 4 of 7 proposals — and not one disagreement was the model being wrong. Two (23, 24) were the derivation working as designed, exceptions list included. Two (07, 18) were genuinely stale records it caught: proposal 18's report shipped on 15 August and its status never moved.

Two corrections the mechanism needs before it works

Derive over the whole vocabulary, not two words of it. The terminal vocabulary is proposed / accepted / completed / completed in part / built / amends NN / superseded by NN. The first draft derived two. built — one of the five it ignored — is the status that lost proposal 01's income model. Every status that reads as finished must be derived, or the rule only moves the hiding place.

Zero declared milestones is unanswerable, not completed. Vacuously, all of none are done. That is the same vacuous pass measured below, arriving through the status rule instead of the checker — and it is the state of all 38 finance-tracker proposals today. A proposal that never said what it would produce cannot have its completion derived, and should say so rather than claim success.

The corpus problem measured in both repos, and it decides D5

The first draft recommended wiring a checker into bin/land, arguing that this repo has built three checkers that sat unconsumed and each needed a second change to give it teeth. That argument stands. What it failed to check was whether the checker could fire at all.

RepoProposalsCarrying a decisions listDeclaring milestones
common-rules1600
finance-tracker387 (5 accepted)0

Two consequences, both fatal to D5 as first written.

In common-rules the gate cannot fire. This repo carries no .common-rules-version stamp — it does not adopt itself — and every gate in bin/land sits behind a test for that file. It has no CLAUDE-checklist.md, no feature register, no ## Milestones table. So the repo that authors this proposal, hosts it, and would ship its checker is the one place the checker is structurally unable to run. That is a fourth failure mode, and it is worse than the three named: not "nobody called it" but "it is called and always skips", which reads as a pass.

In finance-tracker the gate would fire and pass on nothing. Zero of 38 proposals declare milestones. Point the checker there today, with D6's date floor in force: it enumerates 24 accepted proposals, finds no declaration on any of them, and exits 0.

This is not a prediction. It is already happening. proposalcheck reports of this repo that "every proposal that asked decisions recorded them" — and that has been vacuously true for its entire life, because none of the sixteen ever asked. The tool D5 cites as precedent is itself the failure D5 is trying to avoid.

The fix is available and needs no invention. proposalcheck and milestonecheck already share a 0/1/2 exit convention that can distinguish checked and clean from nothing to check. D5 must make that a condition rather than a note beside it.

The caution why the link points one way

A milestone cites its proposal. A proposal is never edited to match a plan that moved. Proposals are immutable records of why; milestone plans are explicitly live documents that move when a state changes or an order proves wrong. Couple them tightly in both directions and one of two things happens: the plan freezes to keep the proposal true, or the proposal gets rewritten after the fact — which is the failure the whole numbering-and-status apparatus exists to prevent.

This redraft is not a counter-example. Proposal 16 has recorded no decisions; its status is proposed. Revising an argument before it is decided is drafting. Revising it after would be the thing forbidden — which is exactly why D1–D6 were left unrecorded until the pilot reported, rather than being written down and amended.

Decisions needed nothing is built until these are answered

  1. D1 — Adopt the link at all. An accepted proposal that authorises building declares the milestones it creates; a milestone row cites its proposal where it has one.

    Recommended yes. Unchanged by the pilot, and the pilot is the evidence: the restructure cost about an hour and produced every finding in this document, including three milestones the plan had been hiding and one attribution that was wrong.

  2. D2 — How a decision becomes a stage. The test is "what must be true before the next thing starts". Revised: three buckets, not two — stage, parameter, and neither.

    Recommended yes, as a heuristic, never as a gate. 19% of real decisions land in the third bucket, they cluster in every proposal containing sequencing thinking, and the first draft misclassified its own worked example. Forcing the ambiguous to one side already produced a wrong milestone source in the pilot. A decision in the third bucket is recorded as such and stops there.

  3. D3 — Milestones without proposals stay legal. A milestone may arise from a review, an audit, or the sponsor directly, and says so instead of naming a proposal.

    Recommended yes. 3 of 16 rows have no proposal, and they include the two hardest rows in the plan. Requiring one would charge ceremony against the most important work.

  4. D4 — Terminal status is computed, not typed. All declared milestones done → completed; some → completed in part with the unfinished rows as the exceptions list. Revised on two points: derive across the whole terminal vocabulary, and treat zero declared milestones as unanswerable rather than complete.

    Recommended yes, with both revisions. This is the payoff — 4 of 7 derived statuses disagreed with the asserted ones and none was a model error. But without the first revision it misses built, the status that actually lost work; without the second it stamps completed on every proposal that declared nothing, which today is all of them. Open sub-question for the sponsor: what does a proposal whose only milestone is next compute to? Not started is not completed in part, and the vocabulary has no word for it.

  5. D5 — Is it enforced? A checker verifying that every terminal proposal's declared milestones are all done. Revised: only on condition.

    Recommended no as first written; yes only if it exits 2 on an empty corpus. The original argument — that unconsumed checkers get ignored — is sound and survives. The target was wrong. Measured in both repos, such a checker would be called and would always skip, which reads as a pass; and proposalcheck is already doing exactly that here today. Wire nothing into bin/land that can exit 0 on a corpus it could not examine.

  6. D6 — Retrofit, or new proposals only? Recommended new only, with a date floor, and no back-filling of milestone declarations into proposals already accepted.

    Unchanged, and the caution above is why: back-filling is editing a proposal after its decisions were recorded. Note the tension D5 creates — with new-only in force, the checker sees an empty corpus for a long time, which is precisely why its empty-corpus behaviour has to be decided rather than discovered.
